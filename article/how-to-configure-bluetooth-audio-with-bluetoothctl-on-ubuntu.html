How to Configure Bluetooth Audio with bluetoothctl on Ubuntu

Configuring Bluetooth audio devices in Ubuntu through the terminal provides a fast and reliable alternative to the desktop user interface, especially when troubleshooting connectivity issues. This guide outlines the step-by-step process of using the built-in bluetoothctl command-line utility to scan, pair, trust, and connect your Bluetooth headphones or speakers directly from the Ubuntu terminal.

Step 1: Launch the Bluetooth Utility

Open your terminal (Ctrl+Alt+T) and enter the interactive bluetoothctl shell by running:

bluetoothctl

You will see the prompt change to [bluetooth]#, indicating you are inside the Bluetooth control environment.

Step 2: Enable the Bluetooth Adapter and Agent

Ensure your system's Bluetooth controller is powered on and configured to handle authorization requests:

power on
agent on
default-agent

Step 3: Scan for Your Audio Device

Put your Bluetooth headphones or speaker into pairing mode. Next, start scanning for nearby devices by running:

scan on

The terminal will begin displaying discovered devices along with their MAC addresses (formatted like XX:XX:XX:XX:XX:XX) and device names. Note down the MAC address corresponding to your audio device. Once you see your device, you can turn off scanning:

scan off

Step 4: Pair, Trust, and Connect the Device

Replace XX:XX:XX:XX:XX:XX with your device's actual MAC address for the following commands.

  1. Pair the device: 
    pair XX:XX:XX:XX:XX:XX
  2. Trust the device (allows automatic reconnection in the future): 
    trust XX:XX:XX:XX:XX:XX
  3. Connect to the device: 
    connect XX:XX:XX:XX:XX:XX

Upon a successful connection, the prompt will display [Device Name]# instead of [bluetooth]#. Exit the utility by typing:

exit

Step 5: Verify Audio Output Configuration

After connecting, Ubuntu should automatically route sound to your Bluetooth device. To verify or select the correct audio profile (such as high-fidelity A2DP for music playback versus hands-free HFP/HSP for calls), list your audio sinks using PipeWire or PulseAudio commands:

pactl list sinks short

If necessary, set your Bluetooth device as the default audio output sink using:

pactl set-default-sink <sink_name_or_index>
