Purpose of Hosting Jitsi Meet on Ubuntu

Hosting Jitsi Meet on an Ubuntu server provides organizations and individuals with an open-source, fully self-hosted video conferencing platform designed for maximum privacy, control, and cost efficiency. By self-hosting Jitsi Meet on Ubuntu, users eliminate reliance on commercial third-party conferencing services, ensuring that all audio, video, and chat data remain completely within their own infrastructure. This setup delivers enterprise-grade virtual meetings while leveraging Ubuntu’s stability, security, and broad software ecosystem.

Data Privacy and Sovereignty

The primary purpose of running Jitsi Meet on a private Ubuntu server is complete data sovereignty. Traditional cloud-based meeting platforms store metadata, meeting histories, and potentially recordings on proprietary third-party servers. Hosting Jitsi internally ensures that end-to-end communication remains strictly between meeting participants and your private server. This architecture is vital for healthcare, legal, financial, and government organizations that must adhere to stringent compliance regulations such as GDPR or HIPAA.

Cost Efficiency Without Subscription Limits

Unlike SaaS platforms that charge recurring monthly fees per host or limit meeting duration, Jitsi Meet hosted on Ubuntu has no software licensing costs. The system allows an unlimited number of users, hosts, and concurrent conferences, constrained only by the server's available hardware specifications (CPU, RAM) and network bandwidth. This makes it a scalable, budget-friendly alternative for expanding organizations.

Complete Platform Customization

Self-hosting Jitsi Meet unlocks complete control over the application's interface and functionality. Administrators can:

  • Rebrand the platform with custom logos, themes, and domain names.
  • Integrate authentication systems like LDAP or JWT to restrict room creation to authorized users.
  • Extend features by installing Jitsi components such as Jibri for local stream recording and Jigasi for SIP telephony dial-in access.
  • Integrate video calling directly into external open-source platforms like Nextcloud or Matrix.

Why Ubuntu is the Standard Host

Ubuntu Server, particularly Long Term Support (LTS) releases, is the reference operating system for Jitsi Meet deployments. The Jitsi development team maintains official Debian/Ubuntu package repositories, allowing rapid installation via the standard apt package manager alongside automated SSL certificate generation via Let's Encrypt. Ubuntu’s lightweight footprint, long-term security patching, and predictable performance provide an ideal foundation for high-bandwidth, real-time WebRTC communications.
