What Is the Ubuntu Community Council?

This article provides an overview of the Ubuntu Community Council, exploring its primary purpose, key responsibilities, governance structure, and how it differs from other governing bodies within the Ubuntu ecosystem.

The Ubuntu Community Council (CC) is the primary governing body responsible for the non-technical aspects of the global Ubuntu community. While Canonical and the Ubuntu Technical Board manage software development, infrastructure, and package maintenance, the Community Council focuses entirely on the human element—the people, projects, and processes that make up the global contributor base.

Primary Purpose and Objectives

The fundamental purpose of the Ubuntu Community Council is to foster a healthy, inclusive, and collaborative environment for all community members. Its main objectives include:

  • Enforcing the Code of Conduct: The Council administers the Ubuntu Code of Conduct, ensuring respectful collaboration and resolving interpersonal disputes or governance issues that cannot be settled locally.
  • Recognizing Contributors: It oversees the processes for granting official Ubuntu Membership, delegating review authority to Regional Membership Boards while maintaining high standards for recognition.
  • Structure and Sub-Team Oversight: The CC approves the creation, restructuring, or dissolution of official community teams, such as Local Community Teams (LoCos), the Diversity Team, and various event committees.
  • Community Advocacy: It acts as a bridge between the global contributor base, Canonical leadership, and Mark Shuttleworth (the project founder), ensuring community voices are heard in high-level strategic discussions.

How the Council Operates

The Community Council consists of community-elected members and Mark Shuttleworth, who holds a permanent seat as the Self-Appointed Benevolent Dictator for Life (SABDFL).

  • Elections: Council members are nominated by the community and elected by official Ubuntu Members for two-year terms.
  • Meetings: The Council holds regular public meetings to review team re-approvals, discuss policy proposals, and address community concerns.
  • Deliberation: When disputes arise, the Council acts as a final court of appeal, working to mediate issues impartially.

Community Council vs. Technical Board

To maintain a clear division of responsibilities, Ubuntu separates technical oversight from social governance:

  • Ubuntu Community Council: Handles non-technical governance, social interactions, team approvals, membership processes, and Code of Conduct enforcement.
  • Ubuntu Technical Board: Handles technical governance, package management policies, release schedules, system architecture, and developer access permissions.

By handling social governance independently, the Ubuntu Community Council ensures that the project remains welcoming, fair, and organized as it grows globally.
