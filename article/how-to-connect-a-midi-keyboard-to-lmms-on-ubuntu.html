How to Connect a MIDI Keyboard to LMMS on Ubuntu

This guide provides a straightforward walkthrough for setting up and configuring a USB or external MIDI keyboard with LMMS (Linux MultiMedia Studio) on Ubuntu. You will learn how to verify your hardware connection in the operating system, configure the proper MIDI backend inside LMMS settings, and route your physical keyboard to virtual instrument tracks to start recording and playing music immediately.

Step 1: Connect and Verify Your MIDI Keyboard

  1. Plug your MIDI keyboard into your Ubuntu computer using a USB cable (or through a USB-to-MIDI interface).
  2. Open the Terminal (Ctrl + Alt + T) and run the following command to check if Ubuntu detects the device: 
    lsusb
    Look for the name or brand of your keyboard in the output list.
  3. Check if the Advanced Linux Sound Architecture (ALSA) recognizes your device as a valid MIDI controller by running: 
    aconnect -l
    or 
    amidi -l
    If your device is listed, the system drivers are working properly.

Step 2: Configure LMMS Settings

  1. Launch LMMS.
  2. Go to the top menu and navigate to Edit > Preferences.
  3. In the sidebar of the Settings window, click the MIDI Settings icon (it looks like a MIDI DIN plug).
  4. In the MIDI Interface dropdown menu, select ALSA-MIDI (this is the most stable and common option for native Ubuntu setups).
  5. Click OK to apply your changes.
  6. Close and restart LMMS to ensure the new MIDI interface backend loads properly.

Step 3: Link the MIDI Keyboard to an Instrument

LMMS does not automatically route incoming MIDI signals to instruments globally; you must assign your controller to the specific instrument track you wish to play.

  1. In the Song-Editor, add or open any virtual instrument (for example, the TripleOscillator).
  2. Click the track name to open the instrument plugin window.
  3. At the top of the plugin window, click the MIDI tab.
  4. Check the box labeled Enable MIDI Input.
  5. Click on the text box next to the Channel input to open the device selection dropdown.
  6. Hover over your MIDI interface category and select your connected keyboard from the list.
  7. Set the channel to the appropriate output (usually Channel 1 or All channels).

Step 4: Test Your Setup

Press any key or pad on your physical MIDI keyboard. The virtual keyboard keys at the bottom of the LMMS instrument window should light up, and you should hear sound output corresponding to the notes played. If there is latency (delay between pressing a key and hearing audio), open Edit > Preferences > Audio Settings and lower the Buffer size slider to reduce input lag.
