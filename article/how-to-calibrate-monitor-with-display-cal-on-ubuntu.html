How to Calibrate Monitor with DisplayCAL on Ubuntu

Calibrating your monitor ensures accurate color reproduction, consistent contrast, and proper brightness for digital media, photography, and design work. This guide walks you through the complete process of calibrating your monitor on Ubuntu using DisplayCAL, an open-source graphical frontend for ArgyllCMS. You will learn how to install the necessary software, connect your hardware colorimeter, adjust your monitor's hardware settings, run the profiling process, and apply the generated ICC profile to your Ubuntu system.

Prerequisites

Before beginning the calibration process, ensure you have:

  • An external hardware colorimeter or spectrophotometer (such as a Calibrite ColorChecker Display, X-Rite i1Display, or Datacolor Spyder).
  • Your monitor powered on and warmed up for at least 30 minutes to stabilize brightness and color output.
  • Ambient lighting set to your typical working conditions.

Step 1: Install DisplayCAL and ArgyllCMS

Modern versions of Ubuntu handle DisplayCAL best via Flatpak due to dependency requirements.

  1. Open your terminal (Ctrl + Alt + T).
  2. Ensure Flatpak is installed: 
    sudo apt update
sudo apt install flatpak
  3. Add the Flathub repository: 
    flatpak remote-add --if-not-exists flathub https://flathub.org/repo/flathub.flatpakrepo
  4. Install DisplayCAL: 
    flatpak install flathub net.displaycal.DisplayCAL

Step 2: Connect the Calibration Device

  1. Connect your colorimeter to a direct USB port on your computer rather than through an unpowered USB hub.
  2. Launch DisplayCAL from your application menu or by running: 
    flatpak run net.displaycal.DisplayCAL
  3. Ensure the software detects your instrument under the Instrument dropdown menu. If it does not appear, click the refresh icon next to the list.

Step 3: Configure Calibration Settings

In the main DisplayCAL interface, configure the target parameters:

  • Display: Select the screen you wish to calibrate.
  • Correction: Choose the spectral correction matching your monitor type (e.g., White LED, OLED, or Wide Gamut) if prompted.
  • Whitepoint: Select Color temperature and set it to 6500K (standard daylight/D65 for most digital workflows).
  • White Level: Set a target brightness between 100 cd/m² and 140 cd/m² depending on your room lighting (120 cd/m² is standard).
  • Tone Curve: Select Gamma 2.2 or sRGB.

Step 4: Run Interactive Display Adjustment

  1. Click Calibrate & profile at the bottom of the window.
  2. Place the colorimeter flat against the center of your screen over the measurement area shown in the interactive preview window.
  3. Click Start measurement.
  4. Use your monitor's physical On-Screen Display (OSD) buttons to adjust the Red, Green, and Blue (RGB) gain controls until the indicators in DisplayCAL align with the target center mark.
  5. Adjust your monitor's hardware brightness to match the target White Level.
  6. Once the indicators show minimal error, click Stop measurement to proceed.

Step 5: Profile the Display

  1. DisplayCAL will now automatically measure a sequence of color patches to evaluate how your display renders different tones.
  2. Ensure your room lighting remains consistent and avoid touching the mouse, keyboard, or colorimeter while measurements are running.
  3. The measurement process can take anywhere from 15 to 45 minutes depending on the selected profiling quality.

Step 6: Install and Verify the Profile

  1. When the process finishes, a Profile measurement results dialog will appear, displaying coverage percentages for color spaces like sRGB, Adobe RGB, and DCI-P3.
  2. Click Install profile.
  3. DisplayCAL will register the .icc profile to your user account and configure Ubuntu's color management system to load the calibration look-up tables (LUT) on startup.
  4. To verify the installation in Ubuntu, open Settings > Color, select your monitor, and verify that the newly generated profile is selected and active.
